## Background

In a past life, I grew mushrooms at home as a hobby. I started with PK Tek, then went to grain spawn and a substrate.
After an initial success, I had an abysmal failure. So I went back to the start, repeated my first success, then
over a 2-year period, I replaced every step in the process, until I found an easy at home process that worked in
a house with 5 cats and 2 dogs.

For this go-around, I'll be growing Cyclocybe Aegerita, it's great for stir-fry.

Turns out, the approach is identical to cultivating magic mushrooms, so do not use these steps to grow magic
mushrooms, as that is illegal.

## Step 1 : Get Yourself Spores or Live Culture

Spores last longer (3 years give or take); live cultures grow a few weeks quicker but don't last much longer than a
year. If you follow this appraoch, the slightly faster start doesn't get you much since once you have your first
innoculated grain spawn, subsquente generates happe quicker.

I've used [Premium Spores](https://premiumspores.com/) in the past and found them to be quite reliable.

## Create Grain

I use wheat berrie, they are easy to get in bulk:

* Rinse with water
* Soak about 20 hours
* Spread out and allow them to air dry so they are dry to touch, but still well soaked
* Fill a 32-oz mason jar 3/4 full, put a lid on (with filters), cover the jar with aluminum foil
* Pressure-cook for 90 minutes (15 psi), or 120 if using an instapot (11 psi)
* IMPORTANT: Allow to cool several hours before taking out, this allows released mosisture to get back into grains
  without leaving the jar wet, which encourages mold

## Inoculation: Sterilized grain is injected with the syringe assets.

Put about 3 ML of liquid from your syringe into the mason jar. There are several ways to do this, here's what I do:

* Fill a small saki cup with rubbing alcohol (I use 90%, but 70% is fine)
* Use a crembrulet torch to get tip of the needle red-hot
* Douse in rubbing alcohol
* Pierce the filter in the top of the mason jar and inject 3 ml
* As I pull out the needle, I've prepared another filter to immeditaly cover the injection hole

## Wait until Approximately 75% Coverage in the Jar

The Break/Shake: At ~5 weeks, you shake the hell out of the ~75% inoculated wheat berries. This breaks the single
mycelial block into hundreds of individual mycelian strands, transforming a slow linear growth front into a highly
distributed parallel processing array. It accelerates total colonization and rapidly denies remaining space to latent
contamination.

## Grain to Grain (G2G) and Spawn to Bulk (S2B)

At ~7 weeks, time to put the grain spawn either into more grain spawn, for a next generation, or into a substrate, 
to actaully grow mushrooms. Mix part of the grain into a new jar of sterilized grain (Grain-to-Grain
transfer, second generation) and the rest into a bulk substrate bag (Spawn-to-Bulk).

If this is your first time, 50/50 is safe. I typically go 1/3, 1/3, 1/3, but it's a tradeoff between speed and diversity.
While I will not decide for at least 8 weeks from the publishing date of this blog post, since it's been yers, I'll
probably go for 50/50. Also, I don't want too many mushrooms gowing at the same time. At my peak, I had about 16 blocks
of mushrooms in 8 containers.

### Substrate Choice

The "easy button" for pasture-loving variants (magic mushrooms) is vermiculite and coco coir. This requires only basic
pasteurization, and so is less error-prone for the same yeild. But for the wood-loving Cyclocybe aegerita, I'll use
Master's Mix, which requires full serialization. A bit harder, but my home-built Still Air Box keeps the transfer 
clean.

### Fruiting

Two to four weeks later, the fully colonized block goes into a high-humidity chamber with wet vermiculite to trigger
pinning.

My personal production record: 8 generations, 33 jars, and a total of just over a pound of dry mushrooms generated from
a single starting jar of wheat berries and a mere 3 ml of spore solution.
