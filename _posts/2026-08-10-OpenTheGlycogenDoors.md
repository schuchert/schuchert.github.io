## Overview

When you consume carbohydrates, your pancreas secretes insulin to clear circulating glucose into your cells. However,
skeletal muscle contractions activate a parallel pathway to "open the cellular doors" directly. By stimulating
non-insulin-mediated glucose uptake, you reduce the overall postprandial insulin peak, supporting long-term metabolic
flexibility, reducing systemic inflammation, and improving overall glycemic control.

This practice is not a fix for a poor diet. However, combining moderate eating with targeted low-intensity movement
aligns directly with evolutionary human physiology by using physical work to clear incoming fuel.

## Movement as a Metabolic Signal

Two accessible movements encourage muscle cells to translocate glucose transporters (GLUT4) to the cell surface without
relying on high-circulating insulin:

* **Gentle Walking**
* **Slow Bodyweight Squats**

### Optimal Timing Windows

* **Pre-Meal (0–20 min before):** Primes the local vascular bed and initiates sarcoplasmic calcium signaling.
* **Post-Meal (10–20 min after):** Synchronizes peak sarcolemmal glucose transporter availability with the rising wave
  of duodenal glucose absorption.

> **Caution:** Keep effort low. Overdoing it stimulates sympathetic nervous system activation, driving blood flow away
> from the gut and impairing digestion.

### Practical Protocols

* **Pre-Meal Walk:** Take a gentle 15-minute walk before lunch. Activating the gluteal, quadriceps, and hamstring
  complexes ahead of time primes the lower-body muscle mass to absorb incoming arterial glucose.
* **Pre-Meal Squats:** Perform 20–40 slow bodyweight squats while preparing food. Track subjective signal over rep
  counts: aim for light muscular warmth without heavy sweating or breathlessness.
* **Post-Meal Walk:** Take a 10-to-15-minute gentle walk shortly after eating (even around the house) to match active
  muscle transport with gastric emptying.
* **Post-Meal Squats:** Allow gastric triturating to begin (about 10 minutes post-meal), then perform 20–30 gentle
  squats to clear the initial wave of circulating glucose.

In all cases, activating large motor units creates a localized metabolic sink ready to take on energy generated by
digestion.

While this will not negate extreme carbohydrate overconsumption, combining this gentle movement protocol with
mixed-macro meals (carbohydrates paired with protein, fat, and fiber) flattens postprandial glucose AUC, optimizes
long-term HbA1c, and stabilizes energy levels.
