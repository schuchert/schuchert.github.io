## Overview

Most workout routines fail because they rely on willpower. Your conscious mind (ego) tries to force your body to move.
Willpower is a finite resource, and when it runs out, the habit dies.

This protocol flips that dynamic. Think of these movements as a somatic "bootloader", a minimal set of physical inputs
that open direct access to your autonomic nervous system. When you move in ways that target your fascia, you give your
body a direct sample of what it is capable of feeling.

Even if your conscious mind does not notice the shift right away, your nervous system registers the signal. Instead of
your ego forcing a daily workout, your body begins to request the movement on its own.

## The Core Assumption

This practice rests on one testable premise: if you introduce movement that differs enough from your normal daily
habits, your body will notice.

Standard daily routines lock our tissues into repetitive, static patterns. By feeding your fascia and nervous system
novel mechanical signals, e.g., gentle oscillation, grounding twists, and spinal decompression, you create an immediate
drop in internal tension. Personally, after just three days of 10-minute shaking sessions at the end of January 2026, I
was hooked. Ten months later, in November 2026, daily shaking remains the anchor of my routine.

## Getting Started: Layer Your Baseline

If you only have time for one thing, stick to **Qigong Shaking**.

Doing this for 10 to 15 minutes before bed improves sleep quality and quiets the mind much like sitting meditation.
However, do not force a long session on day one. Start with just 5 minutes before bed, and let your body convince your
ego to go longer.

To keep your mind from working too hard, build this baseline in layers over time rather than all at once:

1. **Start with Gentle Shaking:** Keep your feet completely flat on the floor, shoulder-width apart. Gently oscillate
   through your ankles and knees so a soft wave moves through your relaxed hips, spine, and shoulders. Keep it
   effortless and gentle, especially before bed. (If you ever experiment with faster, more vigorous shaking, sandwich it
   in the middle of your session and always finish with gentle shaking to settle your nervous system.)
2. **Add Humming Later:** Once gentle shaking feels completely automatic, try inhaling through your nose and humming
   smoothly on a long exhale. I practiced shaking alone for months with great results before adding humming, so treat
   this as an upgrade, not a requirement.
3. **Add a Hand Mudra:** To create a subtle sense of balance and close the sensory loop in your upper body, lightly
   press the fingertips of both hands together while you shake and hum.

## The 15-Minute Flow

Once you are hooked on the baseline shaking practice, adding the rest of this sequence gives your body a deeper sample
of fascial decompression. Practice each movement for 3 minutes before moving on. Going longer is great, and you can
weave gentle humming into any of these stations:

- **Qigong Shaking (with optional Humming and Hand Mudras):** Feet flat, knees soft, letting a gentle, continuous
  oscillation loosen your entire frame. *(Link to video here)*
- **Foot Sensing with Twist:** Keep your feet planted flat and shift your weight slowly across the soles while rotating
  lightly through your ankles and hips. Focus on feeling the pressure and contact beneath your feet. *(Link to video
  here)*
- **Dynamic Wu Chi:** Stand in a relaxed, neutral posture with knees soft. Allow your body to sway and micro-adjust
  naturally as it finds effortless balance over your feet. *(Link to video here)*
- **Separating Heaven and Earth with Side Arching:** Press one palm toward the ceiling and the other toward the floor to
  lengthen your torso. Gently arch to the side to open the ribs and fascia along your flank, then switch sides. *(Link
  to video here)*
- **Dragon Twists Waist:** Rotate your torso smoothly from side to side, letting your arms swing heavy and loose around
  your waist. Drive the turn from your feet and hips to wring out spinal tension. *(Link to video here)*

> **SAFETY AND STABILIZATION:** Moving stuck fascia and shifting your breath can rapidly alter blood flow and nervous
> system tone. If you experience dizziness, anxiety, or confusion during any part of this practice, stop and sit down on a
> chair. Place your right hand over your belly. Breathe softly and shallowly, pressing your belly gently into your hand as
> you inhale and letting it recede as you exhale. Aim for a calm, unforced pace—around a 4-second inhale and a 6-second
> exhale—until you feel completely stable.

### What to Look For (First 3 to 7 Days)

Pay attention to how your body responds during the first week:

- **Spontaneous Craving for Movement:** Catching your body wanting to shake or stretch without having to talk yourself
  into it.
- **Faster Sleep Onset:** Less mental chatter and physical restlessness when getting into bed after evening practice.
- **Clearer Grounding:** A more distinct sense of weight and relaxation through your feet, legs, and hands.
- **Dropped Shoulder and Jaw Tension:** An automatic softening in areas where you normally brace against daily stress.
