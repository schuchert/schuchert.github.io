For years, I’ve cued people to push their bum to their heels to shift their pelvis into a neutral position. This past
week, I altered the experiment. While practicing the Tai Chi form, I concentrated on extending upward through the
fontanel while keeping the hip flexors—the creases of the waist—open.

This changes the entire mechanics of a neutral pelvis. Instead of compressing the lower back to force an alignment, the
movement becomes one of expansion and extension.

While mechanically simple, this cue might not be readily available if you sit at a desk all day. Extended sitting trains
your body to be excellent at sitting. Over years, the tissues on the front of your body adapt by shortening. When you
finally stand, those shortened front muscles anchor your pelvis forward. To keep from falling over, your body
compensates by pulling the torso back, which permanently parks tension in your lower back. You cannot simply "open" the
front if the tissue is glued shut.

> **Caution:** Enter and exit these movements slowly. When exiting, avoid using the muscles you've just stretched until
> you return to a neutral position. If you experience dizziness, anxiety, or confusion, sit on a chair, place your right
> hand on your belly, press your belly into your hand as you inhale, and let it recede as you exhale. Maintain 5 breaths
> per minute (6-second inhale / 6-second exhale) until stabilized.

What follows is one global warm-up and six passive, long-hold stretches that rebuild compliance in your feet, pelvis,
hip flexors, lumbar spine, and thighs. I have linked videos for each detailing specific safety progressions. Here is how
they sequence to neutralize your pelvis through expansion rather than compression:

0. **Humming & Shaking** – A foundational, full-body warmup that loosens the entire myofascial network above the feet.
   Gentle, rhythmic shaking combined with humming down-regulates the nervous system, driving the body toward
   homeostasis. Committing 10 to 15 minutes to this before passive holds yields immediate structural dividends: tissues
   yield faster, deeper ranges of motion become accessible, and you tolerate longer holds with less mechanical
   resistance.
1. **Pins & Needles (hands optional)** – The transition from global shaking to targeted stretching. Shaking loosens the
   upper structures, but the feet bear the load and remain rigid. This sequence restores tissue glide in the feet to
   allow for plantarflexion (pointing the toes). We use dorsiflexion (feet pulled back, toes tucked under, sitting back
   on the heels) to stretch the plantar fascia and toe extensors. Follow this by gently shaking the feet or tapping the
   heels to encourage fascial rehydration.
2. **Jackknife Blaster Pose** – A massive, targeted stretch on the anterior leg musculature. Treat the shoulder and knee
   as a single hinge. This alignment actively forces the hip creases to open—the exact kinematic feeling required for a
   standing neutral pelvis. Opening the anterior chain here accelerates progress in Hero Pose and begins decoupling
   lumbar tension from pelvic positioning.
3. **Modified (Yin-Style) Frog Pose** – In the video, I demonstrate this standing first to cover knee protection. While
   Frog is traditionally used for the adductors (groin), this modified approach directly targets the deep hip flexors
   and the mechanical capacity to "sit back" into a squat without lumbar rounding.
4. **Hero Pose** – A staged progression for the deepest anterior tissues. If you cannot sit on your heels, start with a
   pillow. As tissue compliance improves, spread your feet until your hips reach the ground. Only when your bum is
   firmly grounded should you begin leaning back. Lean back in stages, first to locked arms, then to the elbows. The
   strict requirement is zero compression in the lower back. (It took me over 3 years to get my shoulders to the ground,
   requiring the preceding stretches to make it geometrically possible).
5. **Twister Pose** – An asymmetrical rotation to target and decompress the lower back. Cross your right leg over your
   left, place your left hand on the outside of your right knee, and twist your torso to the left. Working through this
   rotation on both sides directly addresses the lateral and rotational restrictions anchored around your lumbar spine.
6. **Pancake w/Knees Bent** – After heavily loading the anterior chain in the earlier poses, Pancake provides structural
   equilibrium. Sit in a wide straddle and intentionally bend your knees to disengage the hamstring attachments,
   isolating the stretch entirely at the hip hinge. The objective is to tilt the pelvis forward while keeping the lower
   back perfectly flat. Combined with the Twister Pose, this ensures you have thoroughly mobilized every line of
   tissue—front, back, and sides—from above the knees to below the belly button.

If you have been sitting for years, Frog Pose will not be accessible at first. The initial restriction is often deep
inside the joint capsules themselves. However, committing to three 5-minute holds of this sequence (spaced at least 72
hours apart for tissue recovery) fundamentally changes the resting tension of the pelvis. There is always more road
ahead, but clearing that initial fascial and capsular restriction is the required first step.
